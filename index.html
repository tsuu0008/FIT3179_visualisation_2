Victoria’s promoted state-forest trails offer hundreds of kilometres of walking. But the supply is concentrated far from where most Victorians live—and only a small share records any disabled access.

Scope matters. This story covers recreation tracks actively promoted and maintained by Victoria’s environment department, mainly in State Forest. It does not represent every urban path, national-park walk or informal trail.

How to read the colours

What does a walking-track grade mean?

Grade describes the overall difficulty of a walk—not just how steep it is. It also considers the track surface, signage, distance, obstacles and the experience walkers may need.

Grade 1 · AccessibleFlat, well-formed tracks with no steps. Suitable for assisted wheelchair users.
Grade 2 · EasyClearly formed tracks with gentle hills and occasional steps. Suitable for families with young children.
Grade 3 · ModerateSome short steep sections, rough surfaces or obstacles. Suitable for most ages with a reasonable level of fitness.
Grade 4 · DifficultLong, steep or rough tracks with limited signage. Bushwalking experience is recommended.
Grade 5 · Very difficultVery rough, steep or unmarked routes. Intended for highly experienced bushwalkers with specialised skills.

About the data: Australia’s standard grading system runs from Grade 1 to Grade 5. “Unknown” means no usable grade was recorded.

The takeaway

Victoria has a rich regional trail network. Equal access is the unfinished path.

The data points to two different planning questions: how to protect and maintain distinctive regional routes, and how to give a growing metropolitan population easier access to nature. Better accessibility records—and more consistently accessible trails—would make the network easier to choose and fairer to use.

Data and method

What is included

Trail locations and characteristics come from the Victorian Government Recreation Tracks dataset, updated 20 September 2026. Population and local government area (LGA) boundaries come from the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Regional population 2024–25 release.

Processing

Walking records were filtered from the recreation network. Grade labels and spelling variants were standardised. Each trail was assigned to the LGA containing its midpoint, so routes crossing boundaries are counted once. Geometry was simplified to keep downloads below one megabyte.

Reading the results

“Trail supply” means recorded walking kilometres in this dataset per 100,000 residents. It is not the total walking infrastructure of an LGA. Missing fields remain missing rather than being estimated.
